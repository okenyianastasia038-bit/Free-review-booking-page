# Content Guidelines

How we write for the Free Website Review page and everything connected to it: social posts, emails, review summaries and redesign proposals.

---

## 1. Our voice

We write like a trusted designer friend explaining your store over coffee.

| Trait | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Clear** | One idea per sentence. Simple words. | “Shoppers can’t find your size guide.” |
| **Warm** | Friendly and encouraging, never cold or corporate. | “Let’s turn more of them into buyers.” |
| **Honest** | Say what is wrong and what is working. Never overpromise. | “Fixes you can act on, whether you work with me or not.” |
| **Specific** | Name the exact problem, page or step. | “Your checkout asks for an account before payment.” |
| **Confident** | Speak from experience without showing off. | “I’ll show you what’s stopping shoppers from checking out.” |

## 2. Tone by situation

The voice stays the same. The tone shifts with the moment.

| Situation | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Headlines | Bold and hopeful | “Your store gets visitors. Let’s turn more of them into buyers.” |
| Naming problems | Empathetic, a little playful | “Carts left behind.” “ugh, not again.” |
| Explaining the offer | Clear and reassuring | “A quick look at your homepage, product pages and checkout.” |
| Form and errors | Calm and helpful | “Something went wrong and your request wasn’t sent. Please try again.” |
| Confirmation | Grateful and specific | “Thank you, your request is in. I’ll email you within a day.” |
| Review findings | Direct and kind | “This is the biggest leak. Here’s the fix.” |

## 3. Writing rules

1. **Write to “you”, speak as “I”.** This is a one-person studio. Use “I” for Anastasia and “you” for the store owner. Avoid “we” on the page unless it means “you and I”.
2. **Lead with the shopper’s experience.** Describe what a shopper sees or does, then the business result. “Shoppers leave at the shipping step” before “your conversion rate drops”.
3. **Keep sentences under 20 words.** Keep paragraphs to two or three sentences.
4. **Use active voice.** “I’ll review your checkout,” not “Your checkout will be reviewed.”
5. **Use sentence case** for headings, buttons and labels. Only overlines use all caps.
6. **Use contractions.** “I’ll”, “you’ll”, “don’t” and “what’s” sound human.
7. **Use numerals for numbers.** “30 minutes”, “3 things”, except when a number starts a sentence or appears in a headline phrase like “top three”.
8. **No exclamation marks on the page.** One is allowed in a playful illustration (“bye!”).
9. **No emoji on the page.** Emoji are allowed in social posts, one or two at most.
10. **Never invent proof.** No made-up statistics, testimonials, client logos or results. If a number is quoted, name its source.

## 4. International English

Most readers are outside Nigeria and many read English as a second language.

- Use plain international English. Choose words that read the same in the UK and the US: “online store”, “shopper”, “checkout”.
- Avoid idioms, slang and cultural references that may not travel, such as “no wahala”, “a piece of cake” or “hit it out of the park”.
- Avoid local abbreviations and time references. Say “within a day”, not “by close of business”.
- Quote prices in US dollars with the symbol before the number: “$1,000”.
- When a time zone matters, write it in full with the UTC offset: “West Africa Time (UTC+1)”.
- Use British spelling in documentation (colour, optimise) and keep page copy neutral where possible.

## 5. Words we use and avoid

| Use | Instead of |
|---|---|
| Review | Audit (on the page), assessment |
| Shoppers | Users, traffic, eyeballs |
| Turn visitors into buyers | Optimise conversion funnels |
| What’s costing you sales | Pain points, friction points |
| Fix | Solution, remediation |
| Redesign | Revamp, makeover, overhaul |
| Store | E-commerce platform, digital storefront |
| Clear next steps | Actionable insights, deliverables |
| Free, no obligation | Complimentary, risk-free |

**Banned phrases:** “guaranteed sales”, “10x your revenue”, “cutting-edge”, “world-class”, “synergy”, “leverage”, “seamless”, “game-changer”, “limited spots, act now”.

## 7. Calls to action

- Start with a verb and, where it fits, make it personal: “Book my free review”, “Find my top three”, “Get my next steps”.
- Keep buttons to five words or fewer.
- Every call to action on the page leads to the same place: the booking form. Never add a second, competing goal.
- Place a call to action after every section that answers a question a visitor might have.

## 8. Accessibility in writing

- Write link text that makes sense on its own. Never use “click here” or “learn more”.
- Give every meaningful illustration a short description that says what it shows: “A full shopping cart left behind”.
- Mark decorative doodles as hidden from screen readers.
- Don’t rely on colour alone to carry meaning. Errors use words as well as red.
- Keep form labels visible at all times. Hint text gives an example, never the instruction itself.

## 9. Social and email content

The page is the destination for social content. Content should feed it, not repeat it.

**TikTok: *Good UX for e-commerce websites***
- 30-second videos, one problem per episode
- Structure: hook naming the problem, why shoppers react that way, one fix, then the invitation
- Closing line: “Want me to look at your store? The link in my bio books a free review.”

**LinkedIn carousels**
- One idea per slide, five to eight slides
- Final slide: a single call to action pointing to the booking page
- Signed “Anastasia A.O”

**Review follow-up email**
- Subject: “Your free website review: 3 things to fix first”
- Open with one thing that is working well
- List the three biggest problems, each with the fix and why it matters to shoppers
- Close with the redesign offer, the fixed price and a single next step, with no pressure

## 10. Checklist before publishing any copy

- [ ] Does it speak to the store owner as “you”?
- [ ] Would a non-designer understand every word?
- [ ] Is every sentence under 20 words?
- [ ] Does it name a specific problem or outcome?
- [ ] Is every claim true and something Anastasia will deliver?
- [ ] Does it avoid idioms, slang and banned phrases?
- [ ] Does every link and button make sense on its own?
- [ ] Is there one clear next step?
